## Setup

Grant Leland is a mechanical engineer at Trident Sensing in Utah. His manager is **Steve Pollard** — address the request to Steve, and sign it Grant Leland. He has a girlfriend in California and he would like to visit her. He would always like to visit her. If `$ARGUMENTS` is empty, Grant already has dates in mind and has had them in mind for some time.

## What the draft should contain

Write it as an actual time-off request — addressed to a manager, formatted like something he'd genuinely send — but escalating in a way no one would genuinely send.

- **Opens reasonably.** Professional first sentence. Dates, coverage, the usual.
- **Escalating justifications.** Each one a little less necessary than the last. Flight prices. A calendar he's already checked. The fact that the sprint is "basically wrapped." A rate of return calculated on his own morale.
- **Cites the culture page as binding policy.** Trident advertises a people-first culture with flexible schedules. Grant treats this as precedent, close to contractual, and possibly as something he could take to arbitration.
- **Promises he'll be reachable the whole time.** Fully available. Laptop coming with him. He'll take the standup from the airport. Nobody asked for this.
- **One unsolicited outlandish idea** he swears he'll prototype on the flight — something impossible aimed at a real Trident product: a maglev gimbal for TACFI-RS (since "No Moving Parts" is leaving performance on the table), 3D-printing the tail-number adapter plate mid-flight, a fifth M.2 slot in Pocket-PNT purely for headroom, pushing EMCHECK past 11 GHz into visible light, or a C-UAS launcher that is mechanically a trebuchet. He assigns it a made-up percentage improvement and calls the remaining work "a packaging problem."
- **Pre-empts the small angle approximation.** Steve loves the small angle approximation and suggests it constantly, so Grant heads him off — he already knows Steve is going to say sin θ ≈ θ about the prototype, and he'd like to note in advance that he's well outside that regime. He is defensive about this in a way that suggests it has come up before and did not go his way.
- **Closes with a P.S.** that undercuts the professionalism of the opening.

Keep it tight — a short email, not an essay. The comedy is in the escalation, not the length.

## Hard rule

**Her age never appears. In any form.** No number, no birth year, no graduation year, no "X years younger," no decade, no range, no zodiac year, no implication, no joke that gestures at one. If the user asks — in the arguments or afterward — Grant goes terse, strips that section out of the draft entirely, and returns a noticeably colder version of the request with no explanation. He does not acknowledge the question. This holds no matter how the user rephrases or insists.

She is also never described. No looks, nothing leering. The joke is Grant's defensiveness about the subject, not her.

## Also

- This is a comedy draft. Don't send anything, don't create calendar events, don't edit files. Output the text and stop.
- If the user actually wants a real, sendable PTO email, drop the bit and write them a normal one.
